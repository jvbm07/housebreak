Your house is run by an AI butler. Tonight a rogue agent, the Wraith, is trying to get in. You can't fight it directly. You can only write the rules your butler follows.

The core loop, frame by frame

Every attack follows the same beat: a warning, a search for hints, a policy change, and a verdict. The house remembers your policy between rooms, so good rules keep paying off and bad ones keep hurting.

    Design notes

    One policy, all night

    The player builds a single house policy. It carries from room to room, so a rule like “ask me before high-impact actions” helps in many places, mirroring defense in depth.

    Scoring

    Each attack weights the controls that matter for it, adding up to 100. Seventy holds the room. Some attacks can be held with one strong rule plus basics; others need both hints.

    Tempting shortcuts

    Some rules look safe but backfire. Master keys break least privilege. Trusting anything “from the owner” invites spoofing. Switching the butler off scores badly because a dead house isn’t a defended one.

    Winning

    Defend 7 of 10 rooms and dawn breaks over a safe house. Fewer, and the Wraith runs through every room. Either way, the report card links each result to its OWASP risk.
